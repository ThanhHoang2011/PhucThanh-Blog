🏸 Introduction & Origin Story

Did you know that over 300 million people worldwide play badminton, with China standing as one of the most dominant countries in the history of the sport? My journey into badminton started when I was struggling with competitive chess and felt the urge to try something completely new and energetic.

Seeing my interest, my coach introduced me to badminton. Little did I know that within just three years, I would transition into competitive play and start winning tournaments.

⚡ How Badminton Works: Rules & Basics

For those unfamiliar with badminton, it is a fast-paced racket sport played either as singles (one-on-one) or doubles (two-on-two). While it shares similarities with tennis and pickleball, badminton uses a shuttlecock instead of a ball. The main objective is simple: keep the shuttlecock from touching the ground on your side of the court while driving it over the net into your opponent's court.

🎯 Racket Contact Point & Shot Physics

Striking the shuttlecock at the peak of your reach creates steep downward angles, making smash shots almost impossible to return.

💥 Smash

  • Contact: Highest peak overhead
  • Action: Wrist snap downward

🎾 Drive

  • Contact: Flat chest height
  • Action: Quick push across net

🍃 Drop Shot

  • Contact: High overhead disguising power
  • Action: Soft decelerated touch

🏆 My Journey & Growth

My relationship with the sport evolved rapidly—from playing casually to recover energy to becoming a serious threat in competitive tournaments. Success did not come immediately; in the beginning, I lost almost every match I entered.

However, by treating every mistake as a lesson, I continuously refined my technique and built mental resilience until I began dominating matches.

🧠 Reflections & Life Impact

Badminton has given me so much more than physical endurance—it has sharpened my strategic thinking in chess and improved my understanding of physical movement and physics. I am extremely proud of taking that leap three years ago. Without my coach's initial encouragement, I would not be the competitor I am today.
